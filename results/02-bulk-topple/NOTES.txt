STAGE 02 - TOPPLE ALL GRAINS AT ONCE (NO GAIN)

Git tag: opt2-bulk-topple
Type: work reduction (fewer sweeps), attempted

WHAT CHANGED (stabilize only, on top of opt 1)
Instead of removing exactly 4 grains per visit, a cell with g >= 4 grains is toppled
q = g / 4 times in one step:
    q = g >> 2;  grid[i][j] = g & 3;  each in-bounds neighbor += q;
(>> 2 and & 3 equal / 4 and % 4 because grain counts are never negative.)

WHY IT IS VALID
Abelian property: the final stable configuration does not depend on the order or
grouping of topples. Toppling a cell q times in one step is equivalent to toppling it
once per sweep q times.

HYPOTHESIS
Baseline toppled only 4 grains per visit, so a cell with 4k grains needed k sweeps to
drain. Batching should cut the number of sweeps.

RESULTS (N=1001)
Opt 1:  294.2 s (4m54.226s)
Opt 2:  298.1 s (4m58.094s), rerun 296.3 s (4m56.262s)
Speedup vs opt 1: ~0.99x (within noise, no gain)
Speedup vs baseline: ~2.4x (all from opt 1)

WHY IT DID NOT HELP
- The hypothesis assumed tall piles. In practice, when the sweep reaches a cell it almost
  always holds 4-7 grains, so q is nearly always 1 and the batched path does the same
  work as before.
- So the cost is not draining tall piles; it is sand spreading across the grid roughly
  one cell per sweep, which requires a very large number of full sweeps over a grid that
  is mostly already stable.
- Conclusion: the remaining cost is (number of sweeps) x (cost of a full sweep), and the
  number of sweeps is set by how far sand has to travel, not by pile height.
  (To confirm: instrument sweep count and histogram of q at N=401.)

KEPT ANYWAY
Correct, costs nothing, and helps for inputs with tall piles (large initial grain counts).

NEXT
